# Build the measurement nobody else publishes

Anikhet Mulky's best bet for getting noticed in 2026 is a narrow, technically hard artifact that comes with one number nobody else has published. A polished app will not do it, and neither will another agent framework. The top pick is **`effect-ledger`**: exactly-once middleware for agent tool side effects, shipped with a chaos study that counts how often the Vercel AI SDK, OpenAI Agents SDK and LangGraph re-send emails or re-write rows when an agent resumes. It fits his "agents and the evals that prove they work" positioning, hits a pain practitioners are already writing about, and sits in a gap the crowded record/replay and eval-framework tools leave open. The second pick should come from his hobbies and reach a non-developer audience: a **browser-only astrophotography stacker that removes satellite trails**, a gap no mature tool fills. Three alternates follow, ranked: an in-browser small-model tool-calling benchmark, a "fair harness" AI-plays-games arena with confidence intervals, and a study of how often single-run evals pick the wrong model upgrade. The evidence for this strategy is consistent. Over 2023-2026, the side projects that led to hires, funding or press were small, open and runnable, timed to a capability shift, and packaged around a single headline metric or a 10-second demo. Evaluators meanwhile have shifted weight toward proof that AI cannot fake cheaply: measured results, real users, and documented judgment. **A caveat runs through all of it.** Nearly all of this research came from search-result snippets, because full-page fetches (arXiv, Hacker News Algolia, several news and blog sites) were blocked by the research session's network policy. Many 2026 paper and news claims below are unverified, and they are flagged where they matter.

## Twelve breakout cases share one formula: small artifact, big moment, one number

The documented cases from 2023-2026 follow the same shape. Georgi Gerganov's llama.cpp shipped its first release on **March 10, 2023**, right after Meta's LLaMA weights appeared. It was dependency-free C/C++ inference whose demo was "runs LLaMA on a MacBook" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llama.cpp)). It led to a pre-seed for ggml.ai from Nat Friedman and Daniel Gross ([ggml.ai](https://ggml.ai/)), and ggml.ai joined Hugging Face on **Feb 20, 2026** ([Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Feb/20/ggmlai-joins-hugging-face/)). Peter Steinberger's weekend project, later called OpenClaw, began on Nov 24, 2025, went viral in late January, and ended with Sam Altman announcing his hire on **Feb 15, 2026**, about 12 weeks after the first commit ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-15/openai-hires-openclaw-ai-agent-developer-peter-steinberg); [Star History](https://www.star-history.com/blog/clawflare)). He was not early-career, though, having previously founded PSPDFKit. Keller Jordan introduced the Muon optimizer through NanoGPT-speedrun record tweets and a blog post, not a paper, and joined OpenAI's pretraining team. The claim that the post alone got him hired is second-hand, relayed from an X post, and **neither Jordan nor OpenAI has confirmed it** ([AIbase](https://news.aibase.com/news/18953); [GreaterWrong](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/j3gp8tebQiFJqzBgg/how-the-nanogpt-speedrun-wr-dropped-by-20-in-3-months/comment/uYKcbCx9kddipamrY)).

Several of these cases are skewed by prior fame (Hotz, Karpathy, Steinberger). The cleaner early-career examples are more useful for Anikhet. The Unsloth brothers in Sydney made LoRA fine-tuning about **2x faster with less memory**, published free Colab notebooks, and got into YC S24 ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/unsloth-ai)). Luke Farritor, a 21-year-old undergrad, read the first word from an unopened Herculaneum scroll and won **$40k, then a share of $700k** ([Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/this-21-year-old-used-ai-to-decipher-text-from-a-scroll-that-hasnt-been-read-in-2000-years-180983084/); [UNL News](https://news.unl.edu/article-2)). Simon Boehm's CUDA matmul worklog took a kernel from about 309 GFLOPs/s to **about 94% of cuBLAS**, step by step with numbers, and became a canonical reference that others extended ([mirror](https://gwern.net/doc/www/siboehm.com/d4b3e9d6de0655f490fe6369f4d004936248a833.html)). Reports that Boehm later joined Anthropic could not be verified.

Three lessons for Anikhet follow. First, every case carries a single number or image a stranger understands in seconds: "2x faster," "on a MacBook," a decoded word, a leaderboard record. Second, the projects that won fast outcomes (about three months) were timed to a capability shift. The slower ones, like tldraw's roughly 1.5 years from side project to a **$2.7M seed**, grew through steady build-in-public GIFs ([tldraw](https://tldraw.dev/blog/announcing-tldraws-seed-round)). Third, distribution is GitHub plus X, with Hacker News acting as a validation spike rather than the origin. Viral individual posts in this sample were worklogs or surprising experimental results. None were opinion essays. Anecdotes are not data, though, and this sample has survivorship bias: no counter-examples were collected beyond Pieter Levels' flight sim fading after a reported $1M-ARR spike ([DEV](https://dev.to/promptway/he-built-a-flight-simulator-in-three-hours-and-hit-1m-a-year-in-17-days-then-it-went-to-zero-1b1l)).

## Cheap AI demos moved the value to proof, judgment and users

The evaluator side of the evidence explains why "build something cool" is no longer enough. Recruiters still screen in seconds. TheLadders' eye-tracking work found about **7.4 seconds** per resume, with nearly 80% of that time on titles, companies, dates and education. It is an older study from a party with a commercial interest ([HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/eye-tracking-study-shows-recruiters-look-at-resumes-for-7-seconds/541582/); [TheLadders PDF](https://www.bu.edu/com/files/2018/10/TheLadders-EyeTracking-StudyC2.pdf)). Hiring managers do care about projects. CodePath data reported by Fortune put side projects or portfolios as the **top early-career differentiator (38%)**, ahead of internships (35%), though the survey's methodology was not visible ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/12/18/tech-hiring-slow-but-not-fully-stalled-exclusive-codepath-data-new-secret-to-land-tech-role/)). Trust has eroded, too. HackerRank reports that **56% of developers admit using AI on assessments**, and that hiring managers see polished submissions candidates cannot explain ([HackerRank](https://www.hackerrank.com/reports/developer-skills-report-2025); [HackerRank AI hiring](https://www.hackerrank.com/solutions/ai-hiring-crisis)). Lead-developer hiring rose 22% year over year against 7% for entry-level roles. A candidate under two years out of an MS therefore needs work that reads as senior judgment, not just as "can code."

The replacement signals are named consistently. LeadDev and Augment Code rank judgment, taste and architectural decision-making above raw coding ability ([LeadDev](https://leaddev.com/hiring/how-to-hire-engineers-for-strong-judgment-and-taste); [Augment Code](https://www.augmentcode.com/blog/how-we-hire-ai-native-engineers-now)). November 2026 job posts ask for people who can "explain, debug, and verify what they ship," and YC's Gooseworks asks for "proof-of-work" and lists "Taste" as its core requirement ([HN Who is hiring](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50338728); [Gooseworks](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/gooseworks/jobs/rfgY8La-founding-creative-engineer)). Practitioner and vendor guides for AI-engineer hiring call an `evals/` directory with a results table "rare and impressive" and note that stars measure popularity, not competence ([HeroHunt](https://www.herohunt.ai/blog/how-to-recruit-ai-evals-engineers-2026/); [Modern AI Engineer Guide](https://tonyx1998.github.io/modern-ai-engineer-guide/docs/career/career-portfolio)). The closest analog to Anikhet's positioning is OpenAI hiring the founders of Git AI, a side project that measured how much code agents write. This is a **single-source report** ([nocode.tech](https://www.nocode.tech/article/openai-hired-the-git-ai-founders-to-prove-codexs-roi-the-coding-agent-war-just-shifted-from-capability-to-receipts)).

The founder audience weights the same evidence differently. YC asks for one impressive thing you built and judges "not the type of achievement but the magnitude" ([YC How to Apply](https://www.ycombinator.com/howtoapply.html)). Secondary YC-prep sources stress that a rough product with paying users beats a sophisticated one without them ([Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/what-yc-looks-for-in-startups)). The two audiences reward different kinds of proof. Engineers and lab hiring managers want reproducible evidence that cannot be faked. Founders and investors want traction. The ranked portfolio below therefore pairs an infrastructure measurement with a consumer tool that can gather real users.

## Agent infrastructure still has open gaps where the effect hits the world

The research mapped ten open areas in agent engineering and graded how crowded each is. The pattern that matters for Anikhet: **attack benchmarks, live-web benchmarks and compaction methods are crowded or dominated by well-funded labs. Effect-layer correctness, in-browser agent evaluation and statistical upgrade-gating are not.** Generic record/replay and eval frameworks were already ruled out as crowded. The table summarizes the landscape.

| Area | Crowdedness | Open gap an individual can own |
|---|---|---|
| Side-effect safety on retry/resume | Medium-Low | Exactly-once tool effects plus a duplicate-effect chaos study |
| Statistical regression testing | Medium | Paired tests and sequential trials as defaults, plus a "single runs mislead" study |
| In-browser small-model agents | Low | Tool-calling quality benchmark across WebLLM / Transformers.js / wllama |
| MCP tool poisoning | High (attacks), Medium (defenses) | Tool-description pinning, defense ablation |
| Voice turn-taking | Medium | Open false-barge-in / premature-cutoff suite |
| Prompt-cache efficiency | Medium | "cache-lint" for agent traces |
| Live-web drift, context compaction | Low-Medium but expensive | Longitudinal measurement; better suited to funded researchers |

The side-effect gap has the strongest primary evidence. A Microsoft Agent Framework issue reported that resuming from a checkpoint re-ran a whole turn, sending a **duplicate email and a duplicate DB write**. That evidence comes from the search snippet; the issue page later returned 404 ([microsoft/agent-framework #3938](https://github.com/microsoft/agent-framework/issues/3938)). Practitioners are writing posts titled "Your AI agent re-sends the email on retry" ([DEV](https://dev.to/bzdvdn/your-ai-agent-re-sends-the-email-on-retry-an-outbox-for-side-effects-13id)) and recommending per-call idempotency keys and event-sourced intent logs ([Edge of Context](https://slavadubrov.github.io/blog/2026/05/26/ai-agent-runtime/); [Zylos Research](https://zylos.ai/research/2026-04-24-replayable-agent-runtimes-event-sourced-execution/)). They describe patterns; no shared tool turned up. Stateful SaaS "digital twins" already exist (Pome, Ancha, Mock Anything), so a twin farm would be redundant ([Pome](https://github.com/pome-sh/digital-twins); [Ancha](https://ancha.io/)). The unclaimed layer sits between the agent SDK and the world. It holds the effect ledger, and it is the place to assert on final world state rather than on the transcript.

The in-browser gap is thinner but cleaner. An open Transformers.js issue found **WASM beating WebGPU by about 9.6x** on a small embedding model, and its author's "WebAI Bench" has only one validated path and no maintainer reply ([transformers.js #1750](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers.js/issues/1750)). The search found no benchmark of small models doing tool calls or agent loops in the browser. That claim rests on search coverage, not an exhaustive survey, and WebLLM's own function-calling evals were not checked.

## Hobby niches reward "runs in your browser" plus a picture a stranger understands

The consumer research narrowed the field quickly. **In-browser stem separation is commoditized.** demucs-rs runs the full pipeline in WASM/WebGPU with no uploads ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47234566)), and at least four other open browser builds exist ([demucs-js](https://github.com/bakkot/demucs-js); [Microsoft WebNN demo](https://microsoft.github.io/webnn-developer-preview/demos/stem-separator/?devicetype=gpu)). FL Studio has its own Gopher assistant, and a community MCP server already exposes **90 FL Studio tools** to Claude ([flstudio-mcp](https://github.com/rosasynthesiz/flstudio-mcp)). **In-browser plate solving is also no longer a gap.** SkySolver extracts stars in WASM, and alokm.com says nothing is uploaded ([SkySolver](https://skysolver.app/); [alokm.com](https://alokm.com/solve/)).

The clearest consumer gap is **deep-sky and smartphone stacking in the browser, with satellite-trail cleanup**. eise.app stacks only planetary, lunar and solar video in the browser ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/eise-app)). Deep-sky stacking still means desktop Siril or DeepSkyStacker ([AstroBackyard](https://astrobackyard.com/astrophotography-image-stacking-software/)). Trail removal exists as a PixInsight script, StarlinkSweeper, which is non-ML ([AstroBin](https://app.astrobin.com/forum/topic/260440/pleiades-astrophoto-pixinsight/starlinksweeper-pixinsight-script-that-finds-the-subframes-crossed-by-satellite-trails)), and as academic work. ASTA reports about **0.94 precision and 0.94 recall** with a U-Net plus Hough transform ([A&A](https://www.aanda.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202451663)), and STARLINC, a 2026 preprint, proposes annotation-free training on synthetic trails. STARLINC was cited from its snippet only ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.29145)). The audience is real: Stellarium Mobile has **10M+ installs** and Star Walk 2 about **41M** ([AppBrain](https://www.appbrain.com/app/stellarium-star-map/com.noctuasoftware.stellarium_free); [chrome-stats](https://chrome-stats.com/d/com.vitotechnology.StarWalk2Free)). Satellite trails also give the project a story the general public already cares about.

Games are where the spectacle is. Claude Plays Pokémon and Gemini beating Pokémon Blue drew TechCrunch coverage and a Sundar Pichai post ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/03/googles-gemini-has-beaten-pokemon-blue-with-a-little-help)). The Gemini run's own operator warned "please don't consider this a benchmark" because the harnesses differed. A reported September 2026 Vals AI Minecraft stream became a meme when the model farmed potatoes after a Creeper destroyed its base. The model name and run length in that coverage are unverified and conflicting ([Dexerto](https://dexerto.com/minecraft/gpt-6-astra-forced-to-play-minecraft-gets-depressed-by-creeper-and-farms-potatoes-for-hours-3409634)). Bring-your-own-agent arenas are a crowded Show HN genre ([HN Showcase](https://hnshowcase.com/post/47149586/a-real-time-strategy-game-that-ai-agents-can-play)). Fairness and statistical rigor are what is missing, and that is exactly Anikhet's positioning.

## Ranked recommendation: five projects, two to build now

The ranking weighs four things: fit with "agents and the evals that prove they work," how hard the engineering is, how crowded the space is, and which audience each project reaches. **Build #1 and #2 back to back** (about 10-12 weeks total). That gives one infrastructure measurement for engineers and founders and one consumer tool for the public. Treat #3-#5 as alternates or follow-ons. Before committing to any of them, spend a day checking the prior art flagged under each.

### 1. effect-ledger: exactly-once side effects for agents, plus the "how often does your framework double-send?" study

**Scope.** effect-ledger is a TypeScript and Python middleware that wraps tool definitions for the Vercel AI SDK, OpenAI Agents SDK and LangGraph. It derives a deterministic idempotency key from session and tool-call IDs, writes intent to an outbox before the effect and the result after it, and switches between real, twin (Pome-compatible) and dry-run modes. It also adds **world-state assertions** ("exactly one email to X, one row in Y"). The headline deliverable is a chaos harness that kills the process mid-turn at random points and counts duplicate effects under each framework's built-in checkpointing, with and without the ledger.

**Audience and why.** Founders and engineers at agent companies get a fix for a production bug they recognize. Recruiters get a resume line with a number. Hiring managers get evidence of distributed-systems judgment (outbox, idempotency, event sourcing), the "senior-like" signal early-career candidates need. **Prior art and gap.** Pome, Ancha and others offer twins, and blogs describe the patterns ([Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/agent-rollback-checkpoint-patterns-2026-engineering-reference)). No SDK-agnostic effect layer or cross-framework duplicate-effect measurement turned up. **Check first:** Temporal and other durable-execution vendors' agent offerings, plus the LangGraph and OpenAI Agents SDK issue trackers, which the research did not cover. **Headline metric.** A table titled "Framework X duplicated N% of side effects across 1,000 crash-resume trials; with effect-ledger, 0." The demo is a 15-second terminal recording of an agent crashing mid-turn and resuming, with a counter showing "emails sent: 2" on the left and "emails sent: 1" on the right.

| Week | Work |
|---|---|
| 1 | Reproduce duplicate-effect bugs in all three SDKs with a toy email/DB/webhook agent; confirm prior art; write the failure taxonomy |
| 2 | Chaos harness: process-kill injection, trial runner, world-state counters; baseline numbers |
| 3 | effect-ledger core in TS (keys, outbox on SQLite/Postgres, modes); Vercel AI SDK adapter |
| 4 | OpenAI Agents SDK and LangGraph (Python) adapters; world-state assertion API |
| 5 | Full 1,000-trial runs; publish dataset; worklog-style write-up with every failure mode |
| 6 | Launch (Show HN, X thread tagging SDK maintainers); open issues/PRs upstream with reproductions |

### 2. Trailless: a browser-only deep-sky stacker with satellite-trail removal

**Scope.** Trailless is a WASM/WebGPU PWA. Drop in 20-100 phone or camera frames and get back a registered, stacked, trail-free Milky Way or deep-sky image, with nothing uploaded. Under the hood it does star detection and registration (triangle or quad matching, with an optional plate-solve pass reusing ideas from AstroAgent), sigma-clipped stacking, and per-frame trail masking. The masking starts with a Hough baseline and moves to a small segmentation model trained on synthetic trails, following STARLINC's approach. RAW decoding is a stretch goal; start with JPEG/TIFF/FITS.

**Audience and why.** The general public and the astrophotography community see a before/after image that explains itself. The HN crowd gets a "runs entirely in your browser" engineering story, a hook that recurs across demucs-rs, eise.app and the alokm solver. Recruiters get proof that Anikhet does real systems and performance work, not only LLM glue, which separates him from portfolios full of AI wrappers ([Danial Amin](https://danial-amin.github.io/pages/articles/2026-01-06-portfolio-projects.html)). **Prior art and gap.** eise.app covers planetary stacking only, Siril and DSS are desktop-only, and trail removal lives in PixInsight scripts and papers. "Star Trail Stacker" claims a deep-sky browser workspace but has no independent reviews ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/star-trail-stacker/about/)), so audit it first. **Headline metric.** "50 frames to a stacked, trail-free image in under X seconds on a mid-range laptop, 0 bytes uploaded," plus trail-mask precision/recall on a labeled set, benchmarked against StarlinkSweeper and ASTA's reported ~0.94/0.94. Anikhet's own astrophotography supplies both the demo images and real test data. This project also gives him a reason to seek real users on AstroBin and r/astrophotography, which an infra library does not.

| Week | Work |
|---|---|
| 1 | Collect/label own frame sets; Python reference pipeline (detect, register, stack) to set quality targets |
| 2 | Port detection + registration to Rust/C++ to WASM; WebWorker pipeline; first browser stack |
| 3 | WebGPU sigma-clip stacking; memory strategy for 50+ frames; timing benchmarks |
| 4 | Trail masking: Hough baseline, then a small model trained on synthetic trails, exported to ONNX/WebGPU |
| 5 | PWA polish, offline mode, before/after slider; precision/recall eval and published test set |
| 6 | Launch on AstroBin forum, r/astrophotography, Show HN; collect user frames that break it and post a follow-up |

### 3. BrowserToolBench: can a 3B model in your browser actually call tools?

This is a static page anyone can open. It runs a fixed BFCL-style suite of single, parallel and multi-step tool calls on 3-5 small models across WebLLM, Transformers.js and wllama, and measures call validity, task success, time-to-first-token, tok/s and memory. Devices can submit results to a crowd-sourced leaderboard. It suits Anikhet's TypeScript depth, carries little crowding risk, and every visitor generates data, a built-in distribution loop. The prior art is the incomplete WebAI Bench in [transformers.js #1750](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers.js/issues/1750) and an unmeasured WebGPU tool-calling post ([martinuke0](https://martinuke0.github.io/posts/2026-03-16-beyond-the-chatbox-implementing-local-agentic-workflows-with-small-language-models-and-webgpu/)). Check WebLLM's own function-calling evals first. The headline is a chart: "valid tool calls by model x runtime x device, from N crowd-sourced devices," plus a finding such as when WASM beats WebGPU. Weeks 1-2 build the harness and suite; weeks 3-4 cover runtime adapters and the results backend; week 5 adds a hybrid local/cloud routing experiment with cost savings; week 6 is launch and the write-up. It ranks below #1 because the gap evidence is a single issue thread, and below #2 because it reaches only developers.

### 4. Fair Arena: AI-plays-games with a frozen harness and confidence intervals

This is an open-source harness in which frontier and local models play the same game (a roguelike or emulated classic) under identical tools, prompts and budgets. Each model runs k≥10 times, and results are reported with pass^k and Wilson intervals on a public leaderboard. Anikhet's own Echos engine auto-cuts the funniest or most dramatic reasoning moments into short-form clips. It reaches the widest general audience of any option. It also turns the existing controversy, that harness differences make Pokémon comparisons meaningless ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/03/googles-gemini-has-beaten-pokemon-blue-with-a-little-help)), into Anikhet's evals message, and it repurposes Echos and his neuroevolution background. The risks are API cost (budget it before starting) and a crowded concept, with VideoGameBench, LMGame-Bench and arena Show HNs already out ([SpeedrunBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.08076)). Launch within days of a major model release to get the timing multiplier the case studies show. The headline: "Model A vs B over 10 runs each: A's win is inside the noise," shown in a clip. Plan: weeks 1-2 harness and game adapter; week 3 repeated runs and stats; week 4 Echos clip pipeline and leaderboard site; weeks 5-6 a launch timed to a release, then a stream.

### 5. "Single-run evals lie": an empirical study plus a significance gate

Take 3-4 public agent tasks and run successive model versions from 2-3 providers with k≥10. Publish how often a single-run comparison would have chosen the wrong upgrade, alongside a small CLI that reads promptfoo or Inspect output, runs paired bootstrap/McNemar tests, uses **sequential trial allocation** to stop spending once a result is decided (no source found offering this), and lists "newly broken" rows. The motivating data is strong: τ-bench showed GPT-4o falling from **61.2% at k=1 to under 25% at k=8** on retail ([LaunchDarkly](https://launchdarkly.com/blog/measuring-agent-consistency-with-passk); [τ-bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.12045)), and with n=100 the 95% interval is roughly ±7-9.5 points (from a snippet of [arXiv 2611.19614](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.19614), not the full text). It ranks last because tooling already exists ([agent-eval](https://github.com/RudrenduPaul/agent-eval)) and the earlier finding that eval frameworks are crowded applies. **The study, not the tool, is the asset.** It is cheap enough to fold in as the statistics layer of #1 or #4 instead of a standalone project. Plan: week 1 task selection; weeks 2-3 runs; week 4 analysis and the CLI; week 5 write-up.

Music ideas (sample-to-FL-project, hum-to-beat that extends Snoozy's voice work) were considered and left unranked. Separation is commoditized, flstudio-mcp already covers DAW control, and none of them offers a hard measurable claim. They make good later consumer side projects, not 2026 anchors.

## Presenting the work: one number for recruiters, one worklog for engineers

Each project has to work for two readers at once. Recruiters spend about 7 seconds mostly on titles and dates, so the resume line must be the project name plus one quantified outcome ("effect-ledger: cut duplicate agent side effects on resume from N% to 0% across 3 SDKs"). Hiring managers and founders spend minutes and read skeptically, so the repo needs a README that opens with the headline metric and a GIF. Below that, put an `evals/` or `bench/` directory with a results table and instructions for reproducing it, plus a link to a worklog-style write-up in the manner of Boehm's matmul post: every step, every failure, every number. Add a short, honest **"how AI was used"** section. The New Stack reports hiring managers now expect that paper trail ([The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/how-ai-changes-developer-portfolios/)), and LLM-generated portfolio sites and commit messages now read as generic and impersonal ([Simon Willison quoting Tom MacWright](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/24/tom-macwright/)). Write the post in his own voice. Be ready to defend every design decision live, since polished work the candidate cannot explain is now a recognized red flag.

On distribution, launch on GitHub plus an X thread first, and post to Show HN as the spike. Time launches to relevant releases: a new SDK version for #1, a new open small model for #3, a frontier model launch for #4. Tag maintainers when the findings concern their frameworks, and open upstream issues with reproductions. That turns a blog post into a conversation with exactly the founders and engineers he wants to reach. For #2, the AstroBin forum and astrophotography subreddits matter more than HN, and **real retained users** are the metric that moves founders ([Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/what-yc-looks-for-in-startups)).

## Prune to five pinned projects, each with a number

Career guidance consistently favors one or two substantial projects over many weak ones and treats tutorial clones as "I completed a tutorial" signal ([Code Labs Academy](https://codelabsacademy.com/en/blog/github-portfolio-junior-developers-what-to-include-remove); [DEV](https://dev.to/sahil_sahu/stop-building-todo-apps-build-these-3-projects-instead-1ca5)). This is consensus without data, but it agrees with the evaluator evidence. The research notes did not inspect the five weaker projects themselves, so the following applies the general findings to their names and should be adjusted to what each repo actually contains. The **Airbnb clone** and **MicroCart** fit the clone/tutorial pattern; unpin them and drop them from the resume. If MicroCart has real distributed-systems decisions in it, salvage one sentence about them for interviews. The **ROI calculator** is too small to carry signal; archive it. The **Apify scraper** is only worth keeping as a component of Red Rover's story ("data layer for the growth agents"), not as a standalone entry. The **distance visualizer** should be absorbed into the astronomy narrative if it is astronomical, and archived otherwise.

The projects to keep pinned are the ones with users or real depth: **Snoozy** (shipped to the App Store, so lead with any user or retention numbers), **Echos**, **Red Rover**, **AstroAgent**, and the neuroevolution racer as a visual curiosity. Each one should gain a one-line metric and a small eval or benchmark section so it matches his positioning. A portfolio of five projects that each make a measured claim will read as stronger than ten projects without one.

## What remains unverified

## Conclusion

In 2026, cheap AI-built demos have made the measurement the scarce part. Anyone can build an agent; very few people publish how often agents in their framework send an email twice, or whether a model's leaderboard win is inside the noise. That is good news for someone already positioned around evals. His best project is not a better agent but a number about agents that the builders of the frameworks will want to argue with. Pairing that number with a consumer tool born from his own telescope covers both audiences: an infrastructure measurement earns credibility with engineers and founders, and a browser tool that removes satellite trails gives the public and recruiters something to share.

The case studies imply one more thing: the outcome depends on the launch as much as on the build. Most of the breakouts reached their hire or funding within about three months because they were timed to a capability shift and carried one number.
