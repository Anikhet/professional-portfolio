## 1. What recruiters and hiring managers look at, and for how long

### Takeaway
Recruiters still spend seconds, not minutes, on a first screen. Their eyes go to titles, companies, dates and education, not to project detail. Among early-career hiring managers, though, side projects and portfolios now top the list of differentiators in at least one 2025 survey. A project only counts once it survives a skeptical "did you actually build and understand this?" check.

### Cited Findings
**Time and attention (data)**
- TheLadders eye-tracking study (30 professional recruiters over 10 weeks, original edition 2012) found an initial resume screen of about 6 seconds. The 2018 follow-up found an average of 7.4 seconds. These are **older studies** run by a company with a commercial interest. — [TheLadders study PDF (BU-hosted)](https://www.bu.edu/com/files/2018/10/TheLadders-EyeTracking-StudyC2.pdf); [HR Dive on 7.4s](https://www.hrdive.com/news/eye-tracking-study-shows-recruiters-look-at-resumes-for-7-seconds/541582/)
- In the same study, name, current and previous title and company, role dates, and education took up almost 80% of review time; the rest was keyword scanning. Cluttered layouts (long sentences, multiple columns, little white space) performed worse. — [TheLadders PDF](https://www.bu.edu/com/files/2018/10/TheLadders-EyeTracking-StudyC2.pdf); [MarketingProfs 2014](https://www.marketingprofs.com/charts/2014/25190/eye-tracking-study-how-recruiters-view-resumes)
- Time-on-GitHub figures in career blogs run from "30 seconds" (pinned repos, recent activity, contribution graph, README) to "90 seconds". **I found no rigorous study behind either number.** — [Reskilll blog](https://blogs.reskilll.com/github-profile-gets-you-hired-2026-developer-portfolio-guide/); [Underdog.io](https://underdog.io/blog/what-hiring-managers-look-for-on-github)

**Surveys on what matters**
- CodePath survey data, reported by Fortune on Dec 18, 2025: side projects or portfolios ranked as the top differentiator for early-career tech candidates (38% of respondents), followed by internship experience (35%) and public code portfolios such as GitHub (34%). The respondent population and sample size are not confirmed because the page could not be fetched. — [Fortune, Dec 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/12/18/tech-hiring-slow-but-not-fully-stalled-exclusive-codepath-data-new-secret-to-land-tech-role/)
- HackerRank 2025 Developer Skills Report (surveyed Q4 2024 to early 2025; about 13.7k developers, engineering managers, recruiters and students across 102 countries; the press release says 13,372, which disagrees with the report's 13,732):
  - Lead-developer hiring rose 22% year over year, against a 7% rise for entry-level roles.
  - HackerRank calls hiring processes "a mess".
  - 56% of developers admit to using AI on coding assessments.
  - Hiring managers report candidates who submit polished assessment answers they can't explain when challenged.
  — [HackerRank report](https://www.hackerrank.com/reports/developer-skills-report-2025); [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/messy-hiring-practices-tech-developers/744227/); [HackerRank AI hiring crisis page](https://www.hackerrank.com/solutions/ai-hiring-crisis)
- Karat 2025-26 AI Workforce Transformation Report (400 engineering leaders in the US, India and China):
  - Leaders report an average 34% productivity lift from AI, unevenly distributed and magnifying top engineers.
  - About 70% plan to build AI capability through hiring.
  - Skills to assess include agentic AI familiarity, AI-assisted coding, integrating third-party AI APIs, prompt engineering, and evaluating and mitigating AI risks.
  - The report says the most predictive assessment mirrors real AI-native development environments.
  - Karat found that 62% of organizations still prohibit AI in technical interviews.
  - In Dec 2025 Karat launched "NextGen" interviews built on multi-file projects.
  — [Karat highlights](https://karat.com/resource/2025-ai-workforce-transformation-highlights/); [BusinessWire Dec 2025](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251210685922/en); [Karat interview trends 2026](https://karat.com/engineering-interview-trends-2026/)
- GeekWire's coverage of the Karat survey is headlined "AI isn't leading to massive job cuts, but it's siphoning off weak performers." — [GeekWire 2025](https://www.geekwire.com/2025/engineering-leader-survey-ai-isnt-leading-to-massive-job-cuts-but-its-siphoning-off-weak-performers/)
- Stack Overflow 2025 Developer Survey (49k+ responses, 177 countries): 84% use or plan to use AI tools, up from 76%, while 46% distrust the accuracy of AI output, up from 31%. This is indirect evidence that output nobody has verified is viewed skeptically. — [Stack Overflow press release](https://stackoverflow.co/company/press/archive/stack-overflow-2025-developer-survey/); [Stack Overflow blog Jul 2025](https://stackoverflow.blog/2025/07/29/developers-remain-willing-but-reluctant-to-use-ai-the-2025-developer-survey-results-are-here)
- Greenhouse 2025 report (as summarized in search results): 65% of US hiring managers have caught applicants using AI deceptively. An Insight Global survey found 88% of hiring managers say they can tell when candidates use AI on applications. (Both are second-hand via search summaries.) — [search summary referencing The New Stack, Sept 2025](https://thenewstack.io/how-ai-changes-developer-portfolios/)

**Practitioner statements**
- The New Stack (Sept 2025): hiring managers now expect a "paper trail" showing which parts AI generated and which the developer wrote. Greg Fuller (VP, Skillsoft/Codecademy Enterprise) asks candidates directly whether they used AI to prepare their portfolio. A quoted hiring leader says the finished, polished piece is what matters, whatever its format. — [The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/how-ai-changes-developer-portfolios/)
- In an Ask HN thread on hiring signals, one commenter says they mostly look at side projects on GitHub plus past experience. Another counts only side projects that have users or recurring revenue. Several others are skeptical of portfolios in general. These are anecdotal. — [Ask HN: What signals do you look for when hiring? (mirror)](https://hn.nuxt.dev/item/47409463); [Ask HN: With AI written resumes, what are the new hiring signals? (mirror)](https://hn.nuxt.dev/item/44730506)
- Gergely Orosz (Pragmatic Engineer, Nov 2025) describes the tech jobs market as a "junior rebound, picky employers & a tough market". More scaleups and public tech companies are doubling down on new-grad hiring. — [Pragmatic Engineer, Tech jobs market 2025 pt 3](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/tech-jobs-market-2025-part-3)

### Inferences
- Two different readers sit in the funnel. Recruiters (seconds, keyword and pedigree scan) are reached only through resume lines: a project title plus one quantified outcome. Hiring managers and engineers (minutes, skeptical) are reached through READMEs, write-ups and interview depth. The project has to work in both formats.
- Assessment cheating (56%) and AI-polished applications have lowered the default trust in any artifact a candidate submits. Evidence that can be checked (live users, public commit history, write-ups the candidate can defend live) gains value as a result.
- Entry-level hiring is growing more slowly than senior hiring (7% vs 22%), so an early-career candidate needs projects that read as "senior-like" judgment, not just "can code".

### Gaps
- I found no 2024-2026 eye-tracking or time-on-task study specific to GitHub or portfolio review. The 30 and 90 second claims have no source.
- I could not see the CodePath survey's methodology (respondent type, n).
- The LinkedIn 2024-2026 reports on skills-based hiring did not surface with portfolio-specific data.

## 2. How AI-generated code has changed what counts as impressive

### Takeaway
Because building a demo now costs little, evaluators have shifted weight toward signals that AI can't fake cheaply: judgment and taste (documented decisions and tradeoffs), depth (you can explain and debug it), real users and production use, and, for AI engineers specifically, rigorous evaluation (eval suites, measured improvements).

### Cited Findings
- LeadDev (2025-26): judgment and taste are now the most important engineering hiring signals. One CTO replaced standard interviews with open-ended take-homes in which candidates build a light product spec using agents, so the interviewer can see which decisions they make. — [LeadDev](https://leaddev.com/hiring/how-to-hire-engineers-for-strong-judgment-and-taste)
- Augment Code's published hiring criteria rank product taste, architectural judgment, and the ability to direct systems of humans and agents above raw coding ability. — [Augment Code blog](https://www.augmentcode.com/blog/how-we-hire-ai-native-engineers-now)
- Job posts in 2026 explicitly ask for agent fluency combined with verification:
  - An HN "Who is hiring" post wants people comfortable using AI coding agents and critically reviewing their output.
  - Another posting asks for engineers who can "explain, debug, and verify what they ship".
  - The YC company Gooseworks asks for "proof-of-work" and lists "Taste" as its core requirement, naming Claude Code and Codex as expected tools.
  - Sumble asks candidates to highlight side projects and "evidence of how they think and build".
  — [Ask HN: Who is hiring? Nov 2026](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50338728); [Gooseworks founding engineer job (YC)](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/gooseworks/jobs/rfgY8La-founding-creative-engineer)
- Garry Tan (YC CEO), in his open-sourced "gstack" README as quoted second-hand: "The engineering wall has fallen... What remains is taste, judgment, and the willingness to do the complete thing." — [DEV Community write-up](https://dev.to/yimtheppariyapol/the-yc-president-open-sourced-the-stack-he-builds-with-what-it-says-about-taste-2e3p)
- TechCrunch, Mar 2025: about a quarter of the YC W25 batch had codebases about 95% AI-generated. YC GP Diana Hu argued you still need taste to judge good versus bad AI output. Tan agreed that founders need classical coding training to sustain products long term. — [TechCrunch Mar 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/06/a-quarter-of-startups-in-ycs-current-cohort-have-codebases-that-are-almost-entirely-ai-generated)
- Tom MacWright, quoted by Simon Willison (Jun 2026), describes applicants linking to LLM-generated portfolio sites and LLM-written commit messages. The resulting "perfected" applications read as generic and impersonal. — [Simon Willison, Jun 24 2026](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/24/tom-macwright/)
- Eval rigor as an AI-engineer signal: recruiting and practitioner guides call an `evals/` directory, a Promptfoo or Braintrust config, and a results table in the README "rare and impressive". They suggest asking candidates "did you use evals to measure improvements?" as the most revealing resume probe. They also note that stars measure popularity, not competence. Practitioner and vendor guides; an unsourced claim that evals are the "#1 signal in 2026 surveys" is excluded as unverifiable. — [Modern AI Engineer Guide: portfolio](https://tonyx1998.github.io/modern-ai-engineer-guide/docs/career/career-portfolio); [HeroHunt: recruiting AI evals engineers 2026](https://www.herohunt.ai/blog/how-to-recruit-ai-evals-engineers-2026/); [Eugene Yan: How to interview ML/AI engineers](https://eugeneyan.com/writing/how-to-interview/)
- Commonly attributed to Hamel Husain (via secondary recruiting content): unsuccessful AI products almost always share one root cause, a failure to build robust evaluation systems. **The attribution is second-hand; verify it at hamel.dev.** — [Hamel Husain's blog](https://hamel.dev)
- OpenAI hired the founders of "Git AI", an open-source tool that began as a side project to track how much of a codebase agents write, to help prove Codex's ROI. This is an example of a side project built around measurement creating hiring pull. The source is a single news article. — [nocode.tech](https://www.nocode.tech/article/openai-hired-the-git-ai-founders-to-prove-codexs-roi-the-coding-agent-war-just-shifted-from-capability-to-receipts)
- Commentary notes that hiring managers now review portfolios where every candidate has implemented transformers, fine-tuned LLMs and built recommenders, so generic AI projects no longer stand out. This is blog-level evidence. — [Danial Amin, Jan 2026](https://danial-amin.github.io/pages/articles/2026-01-06-portfolio-projects.html)

### Inferences
- The impressive unit has moved from "artifact" to "artifact plus evidence of judgment": a design doc or README covering tradeoffs, what failed, a measured before and after, and honest disclosure of how AI was used.
- For a full-stack plus AI engineer, the strongest distinctive move is to ship an LLM feature together with an eval harness and reported metrics (accuracy, latency, cost), and to run it against real usage. This lines up with every evaluator group above.
- Being fluent with agents is assumed, not differentiating. Showing that you can review and verify agent output is the differentiator.

### Gaps
- I found no controlled study showing that eval-suite or real-user projects raise interview rates. The evidence is opinion and job-post language.
- I could not retrieve Tan's Aug 2026 a16z "taste" podcast content ([a16z](https://a16z.com/podcast/garry-tan-on-taste-agents-and-founder-ambition/)) beyond its title.

## 3. What founders, YC and VCs say signals a strong engineer or technical founder

### Takeaway
YC's long-standing criterion is the **magnitude** of something specific you built or achieved, judged on its own merits, with execution speed and real users weighted heavily. In the AI era, YC leadership frames taste, judgment and finishing ("the complete thing") as what remains scarce. Founders hiring early engineers increasingly ask outright for "proof of work".

### Cited Findings
- YC application guidance (Paul Graham's "How to Apply", an **older classic**, current form may differ):
  - The founder section matters most.
  - The application asks for something impressive each founder has built or achieved.
  - One specific example is more convincing than generic claims.
  - "What we look for... is not the type of achievement but the magnitude."
  - Don't list the startup itself.
  — [YC How to Apply](https://www.ycombinator.com/howtoapply.html)
- Secondary YC-prep sources: founders who have already shipped a working product quickly and cheaply signal execution. A technically impressive product with no users reads worse than a rougher one with 20 paying customers, a point that trips up technical founders in particular. Treat both as secondary sources; the Startup Fortune page's date is unclear. — [Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/what-yc-looks-for-in-startups); [Startup Fortune](https://startupfortune.com/?p=17139)
- Paul Graham, "Great Hackers" (2004, **classic**): great hackers program for pleasure and can be 10-100x as productive. "A Project of One's Own": self-directed projects are both more enjoyable and more productive, and startups and open source share this quality. I found no direct PG statement calling side projects a hiring signal. — [Great Hackers](https://paulgraham.com/gh.html); [A Project of One's Own](https://paulgraham.com/own.html)
- Tan has said vibe and agentic coding let a founder be "400 versions" of themselves from two years earlier, and that founders don't need teams of 50-100 engineers. A Latent Space summary of AI Engineer World's Fair (Jul 2026) reports Tan saying the fastest-growing YC founders treat AI "as a workforce," not autocomplete. — [AOL/CNBC](https://www.aol.com/news/vibe-coding-lets-10-engineers-180804614.html); [Latent Space wiki](https://latent.space/wiki/garry-tan/)
- YC's Lightcone podcast has defended "GPT wrappers" as businesses, through episode 1's chapter on the term and the "Vertical AI Agents Could Be 10X Bigger Than SaaS" episode. NFX argues the wrappers that die are single-task tools betting that models stay static, while vertical depth and network effects survive. These are investor views on companies, not on hiring portfolios. — [YC Lightcone Ep 1](https://ycombinator.com/library/Kb-the-truth-about-building-ai-startups-today-lightcone-podcast-ep-1); [NFX](https://nfx.com/post/ai-wrapper-dead-verticalization-startups)
- a16z crypto has a "Proof of talent" essay on hiring from verifiable output. I could not see its content beyond the title. Most other "proof of work replaces resumes" content comes from vendors (Fueler, Provn) and carries unverifiable stats; for example, an "89% trial-project hire rate" attributed to "YC 2025" is excluded. — [a16z crypto: Proof of talent](https://www.a16zcrypto.com/posts/article/proof-of-talent); [Provn: proof of work for early-career builders, Jun 2026](https://provn.co/blog/2026/06/proof-work-early-career-builders)
- Provn's definition of proof of work is visible evidence that a builder can define a problem, make something useful, explain decisions, handle constraints, and improve the work after reality pushes back. This is a vendor source, but the definition is useful. — [Provn](https://provn.co/blog/2026/06/proof-work-early-career-builders)

### Inferences
- For founders and VCs, "users or traction" plus "speed of shipping" beats technical sophistication alone. Measured against YC's "magnitude" bar, a side project with real, retained users or revenue is closer to a founder signal than to a hiring signal.
- YC's defense of wrapper *companies* does not rescue wrapper *portfolio projects*. The defense rests on vertical depth, workflow ownership and customers, which are exactly what a thin demo lacks.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve direct quotes from YC partner talks (2025-26) about evaluating technical founders' projects.
- I found no first-party VC survey on technical-founder signals.

## 4. Signals called out as negative or neutral

### Takeaway
Tutorial clones (todo apps, follow-along builds), "I asked ChatGPT to build a CRUD app" projects, and thin GPT wrappers are consistently described as minimal or zero signal. They aren't necessarily disqualifying, but they are weak unless they show decisions, real usage or production deployment. LLM-generic presentation (generated portfolio sites and commit messages) is an emerging negative.

### Cited Findings
- Todo apps and tutorial follow-alongs signal "I completed a tutorial", not that you can solve real problems. Guides recommend one or two substantial projects over many weak ones, and define ownership as making decisions, handling tradeoffs, and extending a project beyond the walkthrough. (Career-blog consensus, no data.) — [DEV: Stop building todo apps](https://dev.to/sahil_sahu/stop-building-todo-apps-build-these-3-projects-instead-1ca5); [Code Labs Academy](https://codelabsacademy.com/en/blog/github-portfolio-junior-developers-what-to-include-remove); [Peerlist](https://peerlist.io/whykislay/articles/how-developers-can-show-realwork)
- "I asked ChatGPT to write a CRUD app and pasted it" is minimal signal. Using AI autocomplete is table stakes, not something to put in a portfolio. The trap is shipping an artifact without showing your own decisions in it. — [techinterview.org, 2026](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233475399/ai-portfolio-built-with-ai-2026-interviews/); [vibecoder.me](https://blog.vibecoder.me/portfolio-with-ai-assisted-projects)
- A ChatGPT wrapper is "usually weak evidence" rather than disqualifying. Debrief panels split between "the candidate can execute" and "execute what?". Inflated bullets collapse within 10 minutes of technical questioning. Reviewers want to know whether the project was used by real users and whether it ran in production. (Practitioner blogs and anecdotes.) — [PM blog: Built a ChatGPT wrapper](https://pm-blog-3se-bsg.pages.dev/built-a-chatgpt-wrapper-why-interviewers-still-wont-buy-it); [FACE Prep 2026](https://faceprep.in/article/how-to-frame-chatgpt-wrapper-projects-on-resume-2026/)
- LLM-generated portfolio sites and commit messages make applications read as generic and impersonal. — [Simon Willison quoting Tom MacWright, Jun 2026](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/24/tom-macwright/)
- GitHub stars are a popularity signal, not a competence signal. — [HeroHunt](https://www.herohunt.ai/blog/how-to-recruit-ai-evals-engineers-2026/)
- Being unable to explain polished work when challenged is a red flag hiring managers report seeing more often in the AI era. — [HackerRank](https://www.hackerrank.com/solutions/ai-hiring-crisis)
- Orosz has reportedly been skeptical of juniors pursuing open-source contributions as a strategy, since they can be harder than building your own side project. Third-party summary, unverified. — [ShiftMag](https://shiftmag.dev/the-pragmatic-engineers-career-advice-for-tough-times-4357/)

### Inferences
- Neutral-to-negative: clones, unmodified tutorials, single-prompt wrappers, portfolio sites with no depth, star counts, and vibe-coded apps the author can't defend.
- The wrapper penalty can be reversed by adding the missing evidence: a vertical problem, real users, an eval harness, cost and latency engineering, failure analysis, and a clear note on what was written by hand versus by AI.

### Gaps
- I found no quantitative data (survey or ATS study) measuring the penalty for tutorial or wrapper projects. All evidence is qualitative or practitioner opinion.
- I found no big-tech-specific (FAANG) published statement on how portfolios are weighed for new grads. The general belief that big tech weighs them less than startups do remains unverified here.
